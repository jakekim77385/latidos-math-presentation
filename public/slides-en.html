The starting point · Panama, 2021

I arrived in Panama.
And something caught my attention.

At a café, I paid for my coffee and received change. Something didn't add up. I counted again — it was wrong.

At the supermarket, the same thing happened. Sometimes too little, sometimes too much. It wasn't dishonesty — there was something deeper going on.

Could this be related to how basic arithmetic operations are taught here?

The problem I identified

The previous curriculum moved
too fast

⚡

Accelerated progression

Some content was introduced in very early grades, before students could properly absorb it.

⏩

No time to consolidate

Students moved to the next topic before the previous one was fully understood and practiced.

🧱

Fragile foundation

Without mastery of the four basic operations, everything that follows — fractions, ratios, algebra — becomes much harder.

5 / 12
MEDUCA · 2024 Curriculum Revision

The reform improved the pace.
But gaps remain.

✅

What improved

The progression was adjusted. The Fundamental Learning Rights (DFA) approach was adopted — comprehension first, then advancement.

⚠️

What still needs work

Some content is still introduced earlier than in Korea or the US — and for those topics, there are insufficient practice tools.

💡

The opportunity

The reform is heading in the right direction. What it needs now are digital tools that support that pedagogical vision.

6 / 12
Three sources · One conclusion

This need didn't come from a book.

Personal observation: In cafés and supermarkets, change calculations were frequently incorrect — a sign of fragility in basic arithmetic operations.

Voices from the field: Volunteer teachers and parents confirmed: children need more practice with the four basic operations from an early age.

Curriculum analysis: Comparing three national curricula confirmed the gap in practice tools adapted to the Panamanian context.

All three sources pointed to the same place: basic operations · early practice · accessible tools
